# Ellie Math v2: her world, her choice

Design for making Ellie Math something she wants to open, not something she gets through. Written after a weekend of real use, and a brainstorm on 2026-10-27.

## Why change

v1 works as practice: the fact engine, the honesty rules, the help, and the grown-up side all do their jobs. But for her it still feels like work.

- **The world isn't a through line.** It's a home base she returns to between sessions. The progress it shows is real, but it sits beside the math, not inside it.
- **The fun is around the math, not in it.** The questions themselves look like a worksheet (a number, a keypad, a clock). The world, celebrations and surprises come before or after, so she's being paid in flowers to do the work.
- **What she chooses on her own is creation and agency.** She picks finger-painting coloring books, Minecraft, and Sneaky Sasquatch. They have one thing in common: she decides what happens next, and she leaves her own mark on the world.

v2 turns that around. **Her world becomes a place she lives in and builds. The math becomes the jobs that let her build it.**

## Principles

1. **She chooses.** After the day's required orders, she decides what to do, where to go, and for how long.
2. **Fun in the moment, not just after it.** Every job has to be a small game worth playing for its own sake. A job that's a worksheet in costume has failed.
3. **Building has no math in it.** Placing, moving and painting are pure creation, with no right answers and nothing graded.
4. **The honesty rules stay.** A fact is hers only when she knows it fast on two different days. Rushing and guessing never pay. "I don't know" is always safe.
5. **The engine stays in charge of the math.** She picks the job; the engine picks the facts inside it, so she can't wander above her level.
6. **Predictable rewards.** No random prize boxes, and no streaks she can lose. She can see what something costs and roughly how much work that is.
7. **Never punish.** A missed day closes nothing she already has, and a slipped fact never takes anything away.

## The shape of a day

```
Open the app → her world (she can always look around)
   │
   ├─ Morning orders (required, ~10 min): 3 requests posted by Dot and the townspeople
   │     She does them in any order. Only right, unrushed answers count toward each goal.
   │
   └─ All 3 done → the day opens
         Free play, no time limit: any open job, building, painting, exploring, characters.
         Optional bonus order: harder, pays extra.
```

**If she skips the orders**, she can still visit her world, look around and admire what she's built, but she can't earn anything that day.

## Morning orders

Three requests, each a goal to finish rather than a timer: "Catch 8 fish for the café," "Bake 6 trays of doubles," "Deliver 5 parcels."

- **What counts:** only answers that are right and not rushed move the goal forward. A guessed answer doesn't catch the fish, so the fastest way through is to actually know it.
- **"I don't know"** doesn't move the goal forward, but it costs nothing and opens the same two-step help as v1.
- **Hard days end:** if she's struggling, the orders finish at a time limit (about 12 minutes for all three) and the day still counts as done. A bad day should never become an endless one.
- **What's in them** (the engine chooses):
  1. **Always facts:** whatever she's learning or is due to review. This is the core of the catch-up.
  2. **Big numbers:** place value or rounding.
  3. **A rotation:** school work, Teach Dot, or times-table prep, based on what's due.
- **Bonus order (optional):** a harder request that pays extra materials. This is where "challenge yourself" lives. It's never required, but it's the fastest way to the thing she's saving for.
- **Length:** about 10 minutes, shorter than v1's 15. The engine can cover the daily mix in less time when every minute is spent answering.

## Jobs

Every v1 skill becomes a job in a place in her world. She can do any open job during free play; orders send her to specific ones.

| Place | Job | Skills inside it |
|---|---|---|
| Fishing dock | Each fish carries a number; catch the one that makes 10 with yours | Partners of 10, Number Bonds, Make a 10 |
| Bakery | Fill the trays: double the batch, then one more | Doubles, Near Doubles |
| Market stall | Customers buy and return things | +1 +2, Ten and More, Facts to 10, Take Away to 10, Ten Take Away |
| Bridge crew | Join planks to span the gap; take away to fit | Make a 10 bridges, Take Away from Teens |
| Firefly meadow | A flock lights up for a moment: how many? | Quick Look |
| Post office | Sort parcels into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones; deliver to house numbers along a street | Read the Number, Count On, Which Is Greater?, Neighbors |
| Bus route | Drop each passenger at the nearest stop | Round to 10 / 100 / 1,000, The Digit Shortcut |
| Shop counter | About how much? Make change in your head | About How Much?, In Your Head |
| Workshop | Pack crates of ten and regroup | Trade Tens, Column Add & Subtract |
| Town notice board | Characters' problems, drawn as bars | Story Problems |
| Orchard | Plant and pick in rows | Skip Counting, Rows and Groups, Times 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 |
| Dot's school | Check Dot's work, teach Dot the steps | Teach Dot |

**Rules for every job:**
- **She types or drags the answer; it's never multiple choice** for facts. Picking from options invites guessing, and the clock needs real recall. The keypad and drag widgets from v1 move into each job's frame.
- **The v1 engine runs underneath, unchanged:** fact selection, at most 8 facts in learning at once, spacing, rushed flags, misses traced to their parts, and two-step help. Answers in free play count toward mastery exactly like answers in orders.
- **A job opens when its skill opens** in v1's skill ladder. A closed place is visible on the map with a hint ("The bridge crew needs you once Make a 10 is ready").
- **Help is in character but uses the same strategies and words** as v1 (make 10, regroup, part-whole), which match her class.

## Free play

### 1. Building and painting (the heart of it)

She has a place of her own: an island in the Ocean world, a yard in the Garden, a little town in the Classroom, a station in Space. She places, moves and finger-paints things: houses, paths, trees, animals, furniture, decorations.

- **No math here at all.** If she spends 40 minutes rearranging flowers, that's a win.
- **Everything can be painted** any color, with her finger.
- **Her creations save on the iPad** and are included in the grown-up backup.
- The v1 fact garden (soil → sprout → flower → sparkle) becomes one place she tends inside her world, instead of being the whole home screen.

### 2. Jobs earn materials

Each job pays in its own material: fish from the dock, bread from the bakery, planks from the bridge crew, stamps from the post office, and so on. Things she can build cost one or two kinds. That gently pulls her toward variety without forcing it.

- Only right, unrushed answers pay. "I don't know" pays nothing and costs nothing.
- The engine picks the facts, so grinding the job she finds easiest still serves up what she needs.

### 3. Mastery opens up the map

When a group of facts becomes hers, a new area opens, like the beach, the forest or a second island, with new things to build and a new job. This is the through line v1 is missing: learning facts is literally how her world gets bigger.

### 4. Characters with small, ongoing wants

A few townspeople and animals want things: a neighbor wants a bakery, a turtle needs a pond. Each want takes a few days of earning and building. When she finishes one, something happens: the character moves in, throws a party, or tells her a secret. There's no long script, just a reason to come back on day 3 that isn't "do your math."

### Deliberately left out at first

- **Math-flavored building toys** (planting a 3×4 garden to learn arrays). They blur building and jobs back together; building stays free.
- **A time cap on free play.** Once the orders are done, she can stay as long as she wants. Engagement with the app is the point.

## Economy

- **One day of orders buys something small she cares about.** A big item (a treehouse, a bakery) takes about a week of saving.
- **Prices are shown up front.** She can see that the treehouse costs 40 planks and roughly what that means.
- **No random rewards, no loot boxes, no streaks** to lose. Milestones from v1 (the butterfly at 5 facts, the rocket at 50) stay as fixed, known surprises.
- **Nothing she built is ever taken away.** A slipped fact shows up as something to practice, not as lost stuff.

## Dot

Dot stays her guide: posting the orders, cheering at the big moments, appearing in jobs, and running Dot's school. Dot speaks with the recorded "Ana" voice. New lines go through the existing pipeline (`tools/voice`); Azure's free tier covers it easily. Parent voice clips still play at the big moments.

## The grown-up side

Mostly unchanged: the weekly view, this week's 3 things, the parent check, catch-up targets and backup. Changes:

- **Orders:** done or skipped each day, and how long they took.
- **Free play:** minutes per day, and which jobs she chose. Her choices tell you what she finds fun.
- **Settings:** order length (default about 10 minutes) and the time limit for hard days.
- Fast and slow percentages still count every fact answer, from orders and free play alike.

## What stays, what changes

| Stays from v1 | Changes |
|---|---|
| Fact engine, spacing, honesty rules | Home screen becomes her world map |
| Placement (first-day "what do you know") | Daily sessions become 3 orders plus free play |
| Two-step help, strategies, class words | Questions move inside jobs |
| Dot and the recorded voice | The fact garden becomes one place in her world |
| Grown-up area, PIN, clips, backup | Adds building, painting, materials, characters, map areas |
| Offline, no account, nothing uploaded | |

## Build plan

### Slice 1: test the riskiest assumption

The risk is that a math job is fun enough on its own. Before rebuilding anything else:

1. **A small plot** she can build on and paint, with about 10 items.
2. **One job:** make-10 fishing, running on the real fact engine.
3. **One area** that opens when she masters a group of facts.

Put it in front of her for a few days and watch:
- Does she go back to fishing without being asked?
- Does she build when there's nothing she has to do?
- Does she rush? (The engine's rushed flags will say.)

If she fishes on her own, the rest of this holds. If she only builds, the jobs need more work before anything else gets built.

### Slice 2

Morning orders with three jobs (fishing, bakery, post office), materials and prices, and the first character with a want.

### Slice 3

The remaining jobs, map areas for each skill group, more characters, and the grown-up view changes. Then the v1 session screens retire.

## Risks and open questions

- **Building has to feel good on an iPad.** Dragging, placing and painting are the hardest things to get right in a web app, and they decide whether this works.
- **Art.** Dozens of items, characters and places in four world styles. Emoji and simple drawn shapes get slice 1 going; a consistent art style is a later decision.
- **Storage.** Her creations go in IndexedDB on the iPad and need to be in the backup.
- **Novelty wears off.** New areas, characters and wants keep it fresh, but free play needs a steady trickle of new things.
- **Time split.** With no cap, most of her time may go to building. That's fine; the practice that counts happens in the orders. Free-play jobs are a bonus.
- **Open:** does each world (Garden, Ocean, Space, Classroom) get its own version of every place, or do the places share one look across worlds?
- **Open:** should the bonus order be offered every day, or only when she's close to something she's saving for?

## Decisions made

- Required orders first, then free play: chosen over fully free choice (accountability) and over today's fixed session (agency).
- No time limit on free play once the orders are done.
- On a skipped day she can visit and admire, but can't earn.
- About 10 minutes of orders, set by goals with a time limit for hard days.
- Building and painting contain no math.
- The engine picks the facts inside every job; she picks the job.
