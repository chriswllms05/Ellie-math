# Ellie Math: the first week

## Before she starts (10 minutes, you alone)

1. Open the app on the iPad in Safari, Share → **Add to Home Screen**, then launch it from the icon on Wi-Fi (Dot's voice downloads once, about 29 MB). Ellie Math plays **sideways only**: turn the iPad sideways and turn on **Rotation Lock** (swipe down from the top-right corner) while she plays.
2. Press and hold **Grown-ups: hold** (bottom right of the village). Set a 4-digit PIN.
3. In **Your voice**, record 4 or 5 clips. Three seconds each, your real voice, the things you actually say: "That's my girl." "You didn't count that!" "So proud of you." These play when a fact becomes hers and when a skill unlocks. They matter more than anything else in the app for a kid who runs on approval.
4. **Check Dot's voice.** Dot speaks with a recorded child's voice built into the app. In Grown-ups → Settings, run the **Sound check**: it should say "Dot's recorded voice: 8 of 8 packs ready". The iPad's own voices are still in the Voice list if you ever want one.
5. Check the settings defaults: 3 parts × 5 minutes, read-aloud on, both school-work topics on. Leave them for week 1.
6. Tap **Done**, then tap the speaker on any question later to make sure sound works (mute switch off, volume up).

## Day 1: arriving in the village, then placement (sit with her)

She makes her character, then arrives in the village. Dot shows her around: her house, her empty yard, the pond, Rosa's orchard and the broken bridge. Let it play; tap to move it along only if she asks. Then:

1. **First fish.** Dot asks her to tap the pond. Three make-10 questions; each right answer catches a fish, which sells for a coin.
2. **Her yard.** Dot's **Next job** note (top left) sends her to her yard to buy something with her coins. Let her take as long as she likes.
3. **The note on the board.** Then the note sends her to the notice board: "Show Dot what I know." This is placement, about 10 minutes, on a plain screen with her village blurred behind it.

What to say before placement: *"This isn't a test and there's nothing to get right. Dot wants to find out what you already know so it starts in the right place. If you don't know one, tap 'I don't know.' That's the smart button here."*

- Don't help with answers. If she looks at you, say "What do you think? Or tap I don't know."
- Watch for two things and don't comment on them yet: finger counting, and typing fast without thinking.
- When the card appears at the end, read it with her. Praise the honesty count out loud: *"You tapped 'I don't know' 11 times instead of guessing. That is exactly what I wanted."*

## Days 2–7: morning orders, then free time (sit nearby, not beside)

Each day the village has **three orders**, shown on Dot's note and on the notice board:
- **Rosa's apples** (adding) or **Bea's buns** (doubles); they take turns: 5 right answers.
- **Mr. Pip's pies** (taking away: his coins back), or **5 fish** until his stall opens. It opens when her partners of 10 are strong.
- **Dot's lesson** on the notice board: big numbers, school work, Teach Dot. About 6 minutes.

When all three are done, the village says thank you with 5 bonus coins and **the rest of the day is hers**: building, painting, or more jobs, for as long as she likes. On a hard day, the orders stop counting after about 12 minutes and the day still counts as done.

- Same time every day. Snack first, then Ellie Math, then anything else on a screen.
- Say this once, before day 2: *"Do the three orders first, then the village is yours. When you get one wrong, Dot will show you with apples or coins. Look at it before you type."*
- Stay within earshot but doing your own thing. If she calls you over, answer with a question: "What does the picture show?" Never with a number.
- The one thing to react to live: a rushed guess. Dot says "Whoa, too fast!" You add, lightly, "Think first, then tap."
- Each villager promises a surprise after she helps them three times (Rosa's apple tree, Bea's cake, Mr. Pip's kitten, Mo's mailbox). Let her discover them.
- In Dot's lesson, harder story problems and column math have a **✏️ Scratchpad**: her own paper beside the problem. Encourage her to draw the bars or write the columns there, the way she does in class.

## Every day: the two-minute card

At the end of Dot's lesson the app shows **For a grown-up**. Read it, then:

1. Do the **Do this now (60 seconds)** line on the card. It's picked from today: a quick cover-and-say of facts she just got, or, after a day of counting slips, asking her to show you one fact with the make-10 picture.
2. Praise one specific thing from the card. Specific beats big: *"You knew 7 + 7 fast"* beats *"great job."*
3. Ask the question the card suggests ("How did you know 6 + 7?"). Listen. "I counted" is an honest answer and a useful one; just say "Okay. One day you won't need to." Don't correct her strategy.
4. Look at her village together: what she built, how many bridge planks are fixed.
5. Optional: in Grown-ups → Your voice, **Record a reply** (10 seconds). It plays when she starts tomorrow's practice.

That's it. Two minutes. Skipping it on a busy day is fine; skipping it most days is what turns this back into an app she does alone.

## What you should see by the end of week 1

- She taps "I don't know" without being prompted.
- Rushed guesses near zero on the digest.
- A few bridge planks fixed (each is a make-10 fact she knew fast on two separate days).
- Finger counting still present. That's expected. Watch the "I counted" percentage over weeks, not days.

## Once a week: the parent check and 3 things

In Grown-ups, tap **Start a parent check**: ask her 5 facts out loud, away from the screen, and tap what happened. "Knew it fast" counts as a real review; "Didn't know" sends the fact back into practice. Then read **This week's 3 things**: the pace forecast, her stuck facts with an off-screen game, and whether column mistakes are fact slips or regrouping.

## When to change something

Open the digest on day 7 and look at **Catch-up targets**. If a target is marked "behind" at its week, change one thing: add the 5-minute evening review, sit beside her for the first part again for a few days, or play the off-screen games listed at the bottom of the digest. Don't wait for it to fix itself.

## If something breaks

- No sound: check the mute switch; tap the speaker icon once (iPad needs a tap before it will talk); in Settings turn read-aloud off and on.
- The app seems to have forgotten everything: open Grown-ups, it will try to recover from its backup copy on the device. Make a backup file from Settings once a week to be safe.
- She's stuck on a screen: the ✕ at the top left stops the session and saves it.
